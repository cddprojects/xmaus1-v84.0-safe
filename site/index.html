A guide for Australian citizens exploring different ways to work

Explore online and independent work in Australia

Working from home, taking on freelance projects or building a small online business can lead to quite different working days.

You might want fewer hours, more say over your schedule, or a way to use your experience independently. Understanding the options is a useful place to start.

01 · Your goals

What would you like to change about your working week?

Perhaps you want to spend less time commuting. You may prefer a few regular working days, or the freedom to take on individual projects. These goals point towards different arrangements.

  • A different locationExplore remote and home-based work
  • Fewer working hoursExplore part-time arrangements
  • More choice over projectsExplore freelancing or independent contracting
  • A business of your ownExplore online services or digital products
  • Different start and finish timesExplore flexible working arrangements

Worth rememberingYou can combine some of these. Working online tells you how the work happens. It doesn’t tell you who sets the hours or manages the business.

02 · The options

Get to know the different options

Some options are jobs, where an employer sets your hours and pays you wages. Others mean working for yourself, where you find the clients and decide how to do the work. Here’s what each can look like in a normal week.

You run the work

Freelancing

You provide a service to clients through your own business. That could involve writing website content, designing graphics, editing videos or building websites.

You agree on the work, price and deadlines. Alongside delivering the service, you spend time finding clients, answering enquiries and managing payments.

A project may take a few hours or continue over several months. The amount of work can change.

Project-based contracting

You take responsibility for an agreed piece of work, such as rebuilding a website or organising a business’s digital records.

The project gives the work a defined purpose. Your freedom depends on the agreement, including deadlines, meetings and how the work must be delivered.

“Contract” can also refer to fixed-term employment. A defined end date alone doesn’t make someone self-employed. Fair Work explains fixed-term employment.

Independent consulting

You use specialist experience to help clients understand a problem or make a decision.

For example, someone with experience in business operations might help a small business improve its processes. A marketing consultant might review a business’s content and recommend changes.

Consulting draws on knowledge you can demonstrate. Meetings, research and written recommendations may all form part of the work.

Your own online business

You build an offering and sell it to customers. That could be an online service, downloadable templates or a digital learning resource.

You decide what to offer, how to price it and how to reach customers. You also handle the work behind the sale, including enquiries, updates, expenses and record keeping.

A sole trader is one possible Australian business structure. It comes with responsibility for the business’s decisions, debts and losses. Read the sole trader overview.

You’re an employee

Part-time online employment

You work as an employee for fewer than full-time hours. The work may be home-based where the arrangement allows it.

For example, an administrator might work three agreed days each week, handling bookings and keeping records.

Regular hours can make the week easier to plan. Those hours may still include scheduled meetings and times when you need to be available. Read about part-time employment.

Casual online employment

Some work can be done online on a casual basis, with hours that may change.

That can offer a different pattern from regular part-time work, although it may make income and availability harder to plan.

Casual employment has its own rules and entitlements. It’s an employment arrangement, not another name for freelancing. Read about casual employment.

04 · Independence

How much independence would you have?

Working alone and working independently are different experiences. A home-based employee may spend the day alone while following a team’s schedule. A freelancer may choose their projects but still need to attend client meetings and meet deadlines.

Useful questions to ask

Questions about independence and what each means for your day
ConsiderationWhat it means for your day
Who sets the schedule?Some work happens during agreed hours. Other work gives you more choice over when you complete it.
Who finds the work?Running your own business includes finding and keeping customers.
How does payment work?You might receive wages, invoice for your time, charge per project or earn revenue from sales.
What happens between projects?Time spent quoting, marketing and managing the business may not generate income directly.
Who handles the administration?Independent work can include agreements, invoicing, expenses and business records.

Keep in mindFreelancing and business ownership may give you more control over your offering. They also give you more to manage.

05 · A first step

Start with something you already know

You don’t have to decide your whole future at once. Begin with a task you can already do well.

If you’ve organised bookings and records, online administration may be worth exploring. If colleagues regularly ask you to improve their presentations, you could look into presentation design.

Then make the idea specific

  1. What would you deliver?
  2. Who would find it useful?
  3. What experience can you show?
  4. How much time would the work take?
  5. What would you need to learn?

A small, clear service is easier to understand and evaluate than a broad promise to “help with anything”.

06 · FAQs

A few things you may be wondering

Short answers to common questions. Details always depend on your own circumstances.

Is part-time work the same as freelance work?

No. Part-time describes hours. Freelancing generally means providing services through your own business. You can freelance for a limited number of hours, but that doesn’t make you a part-time employee.

Can I work completely on my own?

Some services can be delivered by one person. You’ll usually still have commitments to clients, customers or a platform, such as deadlines, agreed availability and service standards.

Could I start alongside my current work?

A small project or service may help you understand the workload. Consider your available time, and any conditions affecting outside work, before you make commitments.

A “side hustle” simply describes something you do alongside other work. The underlying activity might be freelancing or a business.

Do I need an Australian Business Number?

It depends on whether you’re carrying on a business or enterprise. Employees aren’t entitled to an ABN for their employment activities, and having an ABN doesn’t settle whether someone is a contractor. Check ABN entitlement.

Is work through an online platform independent?

The platform’s terms and the actual working arrangement matter. Using an app or website doesn’t automatically give you control over pricing, customers or how the work is done. Fair Work explains digital platform work.

Can I count on a regular income?

Consider how the money is earned. Agreed employment hours, ongoing client services, individual projects and product sales create different income patterns. Independent work can involve quiet periods as well as busy ones.
